# Briefings and what's due

## Briefings

Before you meet someone, ask Claude:

> I'm seeing Sarah tomorrow. What's new with her?

The briefing has:

- when **you** last saw her (your partner's meetings don't count for you),
- the most recent conversations with their summaries,
- current facts (outdated ones are left out),
- open promises, both yours and hers,
- gift ideas and other open exchanges,
- upcoming dates such as her birthday,
- relationships and groups,
- her check-in interval, or a suggestion for one.

When the summary isn't enough, ask what exactly was said. Claude can fetch the verbatim transcript.

## What's due

> Who should I call this weekend?

Claude asks freundebuch what needs attention in the next 14 days (or any range you name):

- birthdays, anniversaries and other dates,
- promises with a due date (only yours count as yours; your partner's shared promises are shown with their name),
- people you're overdue to see: your last meeting is longer ago than the interval you set.

## Check-in intervals

Each person can have an interval in days: how often **you** want to see them. Intervals are personal. Your partner's interval for the same friend is their own, and so is "last seen".

A person with no interval is never overdue. Claude suggests an interval in briefings once there are a few meetings to go by. You can also just say "I want to see Oma Gerda every two weeks".
